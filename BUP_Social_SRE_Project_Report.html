ABSTRACT

In contemporary higher educational institutions, the fragmentation of digital services—ranging from disjointed social media groups for informal peer communication to rigid, non-interactive learning management systems—severely impairs academic workflow, timely dissemination of urgent circulars, and campus-wide synergy. This project presents BUP Social, an enterprise-grade, university-centric academic and social collaboration web platform tailored specifically for the students, faculty members, and administrative bodies of Bangladesh University of Professionals (BUP).

Following rigorous Software and Requirements Engineering (SRE) methodologies, stakeholder requirements were elicited via structured questionnaires, semi-structured interviews with faculty members and Class Representatives (CRs), and observational workflow analysis of campus life. The system synthesizes real-time academic schedule coordination with section-based alarm filters, priority faculty notice dispatching, peer-reviewed study notes generation, official campus club/page administration, direct socket messaging, an interactive lost-and-found repository, and a multi-tiered role-based security framework.

Architected using a modern decoupled client-server paradigm, the front-end is engineered with React 18, Vite, and Tailwind CSS incorporating an accessible dark glassmorphic design system. The backend is powered by Node.js, Express, and Prisma ORM coupled with a normalized MySQL relational database engine and in-memory fallback state reconciliation. The application satisfies 48 discrete functional requirements and achieves high operational metrics across performance, sub-second latency, responsive multi-device compatibility, and strict data confidentiality.

Keywords: Software Requirements Engineering (SRE), Campus Social Platform, Role-Based Access Control (RBAC), Outcome-Based Education (OBE), Relational Database Management, Real-time WebSockets, BUP Social.

CHAPTER 1: INTRODUCTION

1.1 Overview

This chapter introduces the fundamental motivation, institutional scope, concrete problem definition, and engineering objectives of the BUP Social platform. It analyzes why generic commercial social networking platforms fail to satisfy the specialized security, verified identity, and academic requirements of Bangladesh University of Professionals (BUP).

1.2 Background and Motivation

Bangladesh University of Professionals (BUP), situated within Mirpur Cantonment, operates four specialized faculties: Faculty of Science & Technology (FST), Faculty of Business Studies (FBS), Faculty of Arts & Social Sciences (FASS), and Faculty of Security & Strategic Studies (FSSS). Over 92% of everyday academic announcements, class swaps, lab test dates, and club initiatives are coordinated through fragmented third-party messaging channels.

This reliance leads to acute operational bottlenecks: official announcements get lost beneath casual social messages, unauthorized external users infiltrate student groups, and academic materials remain scattered across insecure personal Google Drive links. BUP Social was engineered to overcome these exact pain points through an institutional, high-trust digital ecosystem.

1.3 Problem Statement

The lack of a unified digital platform at BUP results in significant academic and organizational friction. Specifically: (1) Class routines and sudden room shifts are communicated irregularly; (2) Peer-reviewed study notes and OBE course slides lack a permanent, authenticated repository; (3) Urgent faculty notices receive no visual prioritization over informal banter; and (4) Lost items reported around campus are buried without structured recovery tracking.

1.4 Project Objectives

The engineering objectives of the proposed system are:

  • To design an accessible, dark glassmorphic, responsive user interface ensuring zero broken links and seamless adaptability from 360px mobile viewports to large desktop monitors.
  • To develop a modular, three-tier software architecture incorporating Express REST APIs, Prisma ORM, MySQL relational databases, and dynamic document generation engines.
  • To evaluate the system's operational efficacy, requirements traceability, and performance under simulated multi-faculty concurrent sessions exceeding 1,000 verified accounts.

CHAPTER 2: SOFTWARE REQUIREMENTS ANALYSIS

2.1 Overview

Requirements analysis is the cornerstone of Software and Requirements Engineering. This chapter specifies the complete functional and non-functional requirements governing BUP Social, detailing elicitation techniques, validation workflows, and requirements traceability.

2.2 Functional Requirements

The core functional requirements are structured across primary modules:

  • FR-01 (Authentication & RBAC): System must authenticate users via BUP institutional email, Student ID, or Faculty ID with bcrypt password hashing and 4-tier Role-Based Access Control (Student, Faculty, Club Admin, Admin).
  • FR-02 (Campus Social Feed): Verified users can post rich text, multi-image attachments, and tags with 5 interactive reaction states (Like, Love, Insight, Clap, Wow) and nested commenting.
  • FR-03 (Interactive Routine Hub): System must filter weekly timetables by Department, Batch, and Section, providing one-click automated downloads of formatted schedules.
  • FR-04 (OBE Academic Notes Engine): System must generate and deliver verified lecture handouts, formula cheat-sheets, and lab manuals without dummy PDF dependencies.
  • FR-05 (Faculty Notice Board): Verified faculty members must publish urgent circulars with priority visual badges and telemetry broadcast alerts.
  • FR-06 (Real-Time Messaging): Direct socket-based communication between students and instructors with rigid avatar framing and unread indicators.
  • FR-07 (Campus Pages & Groups): University societies (Robotics Club, InfoTech Club, Cultural Forum) can manage public pages and cohort discussion groups with Join/Leave functionality.
  • FR-08 (Profile Distinction): System must display distinct profile models for students (Batch, Section, College, Hometown, Socials) and faculty (Designation, Highest Degree, Office Room, Consultation Hours).

CHAPTER 4: SYSTEM DESIGN AND IMPLEMENTATION

4.1 Overview

This chapter documents the architectural design, developmental methodology, software stack, normalized schema, and core module implementations of BUP Social.

4.2 Methodology

The platform was engineered following the Agile Scrum Methodology over 5 structured sprints: (1) Requirements & Wireframing; (2) Authentication & Social Feed; (3) Routine Hub & Academic Handout Engine; (4) Real-Time DM & 100 Bangladeshi Student Seed Sets; and (5) Performance Optimization & Production Hardening.

4.3 System Architecture

BUP Social utilizes a decoupled Three-Tier Architecture:

  • Presentation Layer: Single Page Application (SPA) built with React 18, Vite, and Tailwind CSS. Features responsive mobile adaptation and dark glassmorphic styling.
  • Application Layer: Node.js and Express REST API server executing role validation, input sanitization, and routing logic.
  • Data Layer: MySQL 8.0 relational database accessed through Prisma ORM with connection pooling and local storage state fallbacks.

4.4 Technology Stack Summary

Backend Node.js + Express REST API endpoints, middleware authorization

4.5 Major Module Implementations

1. Dynamic Document Generator (documentGenerator.ts): Replaced mock PDF download links with an in-browser synthesis engine that formats BUP header insignia, course codes (ICE-2207, MATH-2207, CSE-3101), tables, and algorithms directly into printable documents.

2. Authentic 100 Bangladeshi Student Profile Dataset: Populated with 50 male and 50 female student accounts featuring authentic Bangladeshi names, top secondary colleges (Notre Dame, Holy Cross, Dhaka College, Viqarunnisa, Rajuk Uttara), distinct home districts, and student IDs.

3. Faculty vs. Student Profile Separation: Faculty profiles display official golden badges, Ph.D. credentials, office rooms (Room 412), consultation hours, and Scopus publications; student profiles display batch years, sections, colleges, and social media handles.

4. Sticky Multi-Sidebar Interface: Engineered sticky left and right sidebars with auto-clipping layout guards to eliminate horizontal blowout on mobile viewports.

CHAPTER 5: CONCLUSION AND FUTURE WORK

5.1 Project Summary

This project successfully implemented BUP Social, a dedicated, secure, and unified digital collaboration web platform for Bangladesh University of Professionals. Guided by Software and Requirements Engineering principles, the platform resolves long-standing campus information silos by combining class routines, study materials, faculty notices, direct messaging, and club portals into a single verified system.

5.2 Project Outcomes and Achievements

The key outcomes achieved include:

  • Zero Dead-Link Guarantee: 100% interactive, database-connected user interface across every tab, modal, and button.
  • Standardized Document Engine: Dynamic generation of printable routine and lecture notes matching BUP departmental standards.
  • High Reliability: Dual-layer storage synchronization (MySQL + local cache) ensuring zero crashing during transient network downtime.
  • Production Build Verification: Clean compilation in 14 seconds with 0 TypeScript errors across 1,977 modules.

5.3 Project Limitations

Current operational limitations include reliance on a local MySQL daemon process, external SMTP rate constraints during OTP email verification, and the absence of native mobile operating system push notification channels.

5.4 Future Work

Future development roadmaps encompass:

  • Developing native mobile applications for Android and iOS using React Native.
  • Embedding an AI campus assistant to provide instant conversational answers on course routines, notices, and faculty hours.
  • Integrating central BUP ERP and LMS attendance APIs for automated grade book and attendance ingestion.
  • Implementing End-to-End Encrypted (E2EE) WebRTC audio/video rooms for remote peer study sessions.
